## How to Use This Guide

This is the onboarding and reference manual for every CharmEd Minds™ team member.
It is organized into **modules**. New staff should read Modules 1–4 (the program,
the team, and the patient journey) before their first patient, then complete the
module(s) specific to their role. Each module ends with a **Knowledge Check** and
key modules include a **role competency checklist** (see Appendix A).

**Legend used throughout:**

- 🩺 **Clinical task** — performed or approved by the Provider only.
- 🧩 **Operational task** — performed by APD, Care Coordinator, MA, or Front Desk.
- ⚠️ **Escalate** — stop and route to the Provider (via APD) the same day.
- 📄 **Document it** — must be recorded in the chart / Notion the same day.

> **Scope reminder:** Everything in this program is built on **screening**, not
> diagnosis. Screening tools identify patterns and referral needs. Only the
> Provider assigns diagnoses and interprets results clinically. Non-clinical
> staff report scores and trends in plain language and never label a patient.

# Module 1 — Program Foundations

### 1.1 What CharmEd Minds™ Is

CharmEd Minds™ is a **lifespan cognition, executive function, and brain-health
program** that helps individuals understand *how their brain is functioning, why
they may be struggling, and what supports can improve daily life.*

The program is **not limited to diagnosis.** It combines:

- Cognitive screening
- Executive function mapping
- Medical review
- Lifestyle optimization
- Education
- Skills training
- Care planning

Built **inside primary care**, CharmEd Minds identifies cognitive dysfunction
early and intervenes with structured, evidence-informed cognitive training and
executive-function (EF) coaching.

### 1.2 The Central Promise

> **Confidence Through Cognition and Education.**

People need *language for their experience before they can change it.* A large
part of our value is helping patients and families move **from confusion to
clarity, and from struggle to confidence** — without shame or blame.

### 1.3 Who We Serve (the Lifespan Model)

CharmEd Minds serves ages **5–90** across **seven population tracks** (detailed in
Module 5):

| Track | Population | Main Program Focus |
|-------|------------|--------------------|
| 1 | Children (5–12) | Learning, attention, behavior, parent/school support |
| 2 | Teens & Young Adults (13–17 / young adult) | Executive function, independence, emotional capacity |
| 3 | Adults (18–64) | Focus, burnout, brain fog, daily function |
| 4 | Neurodivergent Adults | ADHD, autism, AuDHD, masking, burnout |
| 5 | Recovery Patients | Cognitive repair, life skills, relapse prevention |
| 6 | Chronic Disease Patients | Brain fog, care-plan adherence, medical-cognitive stabilization |
| 7 | Seniors (60+) | Memory, safety, reversible causes, caregiver support |

### 1.4 What We Help Improve

Working memory · processing speed · attention · planning & organization · task
initiation · academic performance · emotional regulation · cognitive endurance ·
functional independence · brain-based performance in school, work, and daily life.

### 1.5 Our Two Guiding Principles

1. **Optimize the body before (or alongside) psychiatry.** Sleep, nutrition,
   inflammation, deficiencies, hormones, and medications can all suppress
   cognition. We look for **reversible medical contributors** first — a "difficult"
   or "noncompliant" patient is often cognitively overloaded, fatigued, or
   medically unstable, not unwilling.
2. **Behavior is communication.** Struggles are rarely laziness or defiance. We
   explain *why* the brain is struggling and build supports around it.

# Module 2 — The Care Model & Structure

### 2.1 The Bronze–Silver–Gold Tiers

Every enrolled patient is placed in a service tier by the Provider:

| Tier | Includes |
|------|----------|
| **Bronze** | Baseline assessment · weekly cognitive training (97127) · basic digital modules |
| **Silver** | Everything in Bronze **+** academic support add-ons (97535) · weekly skill-building curriculum |
| **Gold** | Bronze + Silver **+** Behavioral Health Integration (monthly BHI CPT codes) · monthly provider review · care coordination · parent/family coaching |

### 2.2 Core Components (What Every Patient Gets)

1. Baseline cognitive assessment
2. Digital cognitive testing (**CogniFit**, **Creyos**)
3. CharmEd Minds Quick Cognitive Test Kits (Child 5–12 · Teen 13–17 · Adult 18–64 · Senior 60+)
4. Weekly EF training sessions (97127)
5. Monthly progress reviews
6. Quarterly full retesting
7. Provider interpretation & updated care plan
8. Academic coaching (children & teens)
9. Work-function coaching (adults)
10. Cognitive-decline prevention (seniors)
11. BHI integration when mood impacts cognition

### 2.3 The Three Clinical Pillars

1. **Digital cognitive testing** — CogniFit, Creyos
2. **CharmEd Minds Quick Cognitive Test Kits** — Child / Adult / Senior batteries
3. **Weekly EF interventions** — 97127 templates, real-world EF tasks, digital modules, coaching scripts

### 2.4 Standard Program Length

A typical course is **12 weeks** of weekly sessions, with a **quarterly full
retest** and a continuation decision (continue · modify tier · maintenance ·
discharge · refer out).

### 2.5 The Cross-Population Pathway (Seven Steps)

Regardless of track, every patient follows the same clinical logic:

| Step | What happens |
|------|--------------|
| **1. Intake & Concern Mapping** | Identify the main concern (attention, learning, memory, brain fog, regulation, behavior, EF, recovery stability, school/work function, daily follow-through, safety/independence). |
| **2. Cognitive & EF Screening** | Standardized battery matched to age, concern, and track (Module 6). |
| **3. Medical & Lifestyle Review** | Look for body-based contributors: sleep, nutrition, inflammation, iron/nutrients, hormones, blood sugar, vascular health, pain, medications, chronic illness, trauma/stress (Module 7). |
| **4. Pattern Identification** | 🩺 Provider identifies the most likely pattern(s): ADHD, AuDHD, autism-related regulation, anxiety-driven inattention, EF weakness, learning strain, sleep-related impairment, medical brain fog, recovery-related impairment, burnout, MCI concern, reversible contributor. |
| **5. Education** | Give the patient/family language for their experience — the most important step. |
| **6. Care Plan** | Practical plan: skills, routines, medical next steps, nutrition/lifestyle, accommodations, referrals, cognitive training, family/caregiver support, follow-up. |
| **7. Follow-Up & Progress Tracking** | Monitor outcomes by track (Module 10). |

### 2.6 Integration With Primary Care & Behavioral Health

- **Primary care:** chronic-illness management, cognition tied to stress/pain/autoimmune conditions, medication and treatment-plan adherence.
- **Behavioral health (BHI):** when mood impacts cognition → monthly BHI; anxiety/depression → EF deficits addressed; coordination with therapists.
- **Care-coordination programs:** CCM, APCM, RPM, BHI, CoCM where appropriate (especially the chronic-disease track).

# Module 3 — Your Role on the Team

CharmEd Minds runs on clear role boundaries. **Clinical interpretation and
diagnosis belong to the Provider.** Everyone else operates, coordinates, and
communicates — reporting scores and trends in plain language.

### 3.1 Provider / NP (CRNP) 🩺

- Approves cognitive plans and **interprets** all testing (clinical).
- Assigns cognitive diagnoses / ICD-10 codes.
- Performs baseline review, monthly review, and quarterly retest interpretation.
- Identifies medical contributors; orders labs; adjusts medications.
- Bills **96132 / 96136** (interpretation) and confirms medical necessity.
- Makes the continuation / discharge / referral decision.

### 3.2 Assistant Program Director (APD) 🧩

The APD **owns program operations.** The guiding rule is the **APD Ownership
Principle:**

> The APD does not wait for problems to surface. Every untracked patient, overdue
> note, missed session, and unassigned referral is the APD's responsibility to
> find and resolve — *without being told.*

The APD:

- Runs weekly cognitive sessions and administers EF tasks (in practices where the
  APD delivers sessions).
- Tracks scores and **interprets trends non-clinically** (never a diagnosis).
- Owns the **daily referral queue** — no patient unassigned > 24 hours.
- Runs the **weekly documentation audit** — every 97127 note reviewed for completeness.
- Tracks **attendance** and follows up before it becomes a billing-compliance issue.
- **Gatekeeps monthly review** — reviews the coordinator summary *before* the Provider.
- Coordinates **quarterly retests** ~2 weeks in advance.
- Routes **all red-flag escalations same day** (APD routes, Provider decides).
- Audits insurance compliance: billing, consents, documentation completeness.
- Communicates with parents/patients; handles digital-testing compliance.

### 3.3 Care Coordinator 🧩

- Pre-intake prep: reviews referral reason, notes, labs, insurance eligibility; prepares intake packet, consents, testing links.
- Runs the **intake appointment (45–60 min):** cognitive intake questionnaire, hands-on Quick Test Kit, assigns CogniFit/Creyos, obtains consents.
- Delivers **weekly 97127 sessions (30–45 min):** check-in, cognitive exercises, digital module, EF coaching, homework — 📄 documented **same day**.
- Monitors digital-testing compliance (home battery within 48 hrs) and homework compliance.
- Records scores on the intake scoring sheet (Notion) and sends the "baseline review needed" task to the Provider.

### 3.4 Medical Assistant (MA) 🧩

- Provides the CharmEd Minds intro handout (what to expect, time commitment, cost/insurance, coordinator contact).
- Confirms **device access** (phone/tablet/computer for home testing).
- Schedules the **intake appointment** (same day if possible; within 7 days).
- Sends the referral task to the Care Coordinator; helps with testing setup and login troubleshooting.

### 3.5 Front Desk 🧩

- Schedules weekly sessions and the 12-week standing series.
- Sends appointment reminders.
- Supports **attendance compliance** (rescheduling, no-show follow-up).

### 3.6 The Clinical / Non-Clinical Line (Memorize This)

| Non-clinical staff **may** | Non-clinical staff **may not** |
|----------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Administer standardized screens and test kits per protocol | Diagnose or "rule out" a condition |
| Record and report raw scores | Interpret scores clinically or predict prognosis |
| Describe observed trends ("scores improved on the memory tasks") | Tell a patient they "have ADHD/autism/dementia" |
| Educate from approved handouts and scripts | Give medical advice, adjust meds, or order labs |
| Escalate concerns to the APD/Provider | Decide medical necessity or change the care plan |

# Module 4 — The Patient Journey

### 4.1 Entry Points

- **Primary care appointment (most common)** — provider or patient notes ADHD-like symptoms, brain fog, forgetfulness, planning difficulty, poor task completion, trouble focusing, academic struggles, work-performance issues, memory changes, mood affecting function, or age-60+ cognitive decline.
- **BHI referral** — psych provider notes cognitive impairment, adherence difficulty, or mood + cognition overlap.
- **Self / family referral** — website, direct call, or at check-in.
- **School / educational referral** — teacher concern about attention/processing; struggles despite effort.
- **Chronic-disease pathway** — diabetes, autoimmune, heart failure, chronic pain, sleep disorders, long COVID.
- **Vascular program** — vascular cognitive risk.

### 4.2 The 13-Step Journey (Identification → Discharge)

| # | Step | Owner | Key rule |
|---|------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Provider screening | NP | Age-specific questions; document symptoms + functional impact |
| 2 | Referral | NP | Referral note + EHR task to Care Coordinator, **same day** |
| 3 | MA intake steps | MA | Intro handout, confirm device access, intake scheduled **within 7 days** |
| 4 | Assignment | APD | Assign coordinator; **no referral unassigned > 24 hrs** |
| 5 | Intake (45–60 min) | Care Coordinator | Questionnaire, Quick Test Kit, CogniFit/Creyos assigned, consents signed |
| 6 | Digital testing | Patient | Home battery completed **within 48 hrs**; coordinator monitors |
| 7 | Baseline review | NP | Interprets all data; ICD-10 diagnosis + full care plan; **bills 96132** |
| 8 | Plan handoff | APD | Briefs coordinator; schedules 12-week standing series |
| 9 | Weekly sessions (97127) | Care Coordinator | 30–45 min; 📄 documented **same day** |
| 10 | Monthly review (Wk 4) | Coord → APD → NP | APD reviews coordinator summary **before** NP; NP updates plan + confirms medical necessity |
| 11 | Quarterly review (Wk 12) | NP + APD + Coord | Full retest + digital battery; APD compiles report; NP interprets & adjusts; **bills 96132** |
| 12 | Continuation decision | NP + APD | Continue · modify tier · maintenance · discharge · refer to neuropsych/BHI |
| 13 | Discharge / maintenance | APD + Coord + NP | Discharge summary + final care plan; confirm docs complete; primary-care f/u scheduled |

### 4.3 Provider Screening Questions (Step 1)

The Provider asks 3–5 of:

1. "Is it harder to focus than before?"
2. "Do you forget things more easily?"
3. "Is it hard to organize tasks or stay on track?"
4. "Do you feel mentally slower?"
5. "Has school/work become more stressful due to concentration?"
6. "Have you felt more overwhelmed or mentally fatigued?"
7. "Are daily tasks harder to keep up with?"

### 4.4 Training Pathway vs. Investigate-Medical (Step 2)

**Refer to CharmEd Minds (training pathway)** for: difficulty focusing, working-
memory weakness, organizational problems, academic failures, ADHD history, work
dysfunction.

⚠️ **Investigate medical causes first** for: sudden cognitive decline, head injury,
stroke-like symptoms, rapid personality change, severe memory loss, loss of
orientation.

### 4.5 Provider Lab Options (Step 3, as indicated)

CBC · CMP · Vitamin D · B12/Folate/MMA · TSH/Free T4 · A1C · ESR/CRP · autoimmune
markers · homocysteine · hormones. (Routine care continues alongside the CharmEd
Minds referral when appropriate.)

# Module 5 — The Seven Population Tracks

Each track shares the same seven-step pathway (Module 2.5) but tailors the

## Track 1 — Children (Ages 5–12)

**Learning, Attention, Behavior, and Developmental Support**

**Purpose:** Understand whether a child's challenges relate to ADHD, AuDHD/autism
patterns, anxiety, sleep problems, medical contributors, learning strain, EF
weakness, sensory needs — or a combination.

**Best fit:** trouble focusing · hyperactivity/impulsivity · difficulty following
directions · emotional outbursts · after-school meltdowns · incomplete
assignments · poor organization · trouble with transitions · sensory sensitivity
· sleep problems · school avoidance · a bright child underperforming · behavior
that reflects frustration or cognitive overload.

**Core questions:** Is this attention, learning, regulation, or EF? Which pattern
fits best? What is the child's cognitive profile? What does the family need? What
school accommodations may help? Are there medical issues to optimize first?

**Program modules:** (1) Brain & Behavior Mapping · (2) EF Foundations · (3)
Medical Optimization Before Psychiatry · (4) Parent Education · (5) School Support
Planning (504/IEP consideration) · (6) Confidence & Self-Understanding.

**Deliverables:** cognitive/EF summary · parent education handout · school
accommodation recommendations · medical optimization checklist · nutrition
guidance · follow-up plan · referrals · home + school care plan.

**Desired outcomes:** better understanding of needs · reduced shame/blame ·
parent confidence · school communication · clearer next steps · better regulation,
routines, and follow-through · appropriate accommodations and referrals.

## Track 2 — Teens & Young Adults

**Executive Function, Independence, and Emotional Capacity**

**Purpose:** Support teens/young adults struggling with school, college readiness,
motivation, regulation, decision-making, organization, or independence — often
mislabeled "lazy" when the real issue is EF dysfunction, anxiety, sleep
disruption, burnout, ADHD, AuDHD, or under-developed skills.

**Best fit:** poor time management · missing/late assignments · procrastination ·
trouble starting or finishing tasks · emotional shutdowns · school avoidance ·
performance anxiety · sleep-schedule problems · excessive screen use · low
motivation · disorganization · difficulty preparing for college/work ·
ADHD/autism/AuDHD concerns · parent-teen conflict around independence.

**Core questions:** Motivation problem or cognitive overload? Which EF skills are
weak? Is anxiety blocking task initiation? Is sleep rhythm affecting cognition? Is
the teen ready for more independence? What supports/accommodations are needed?

**Program modules:** (1) EF Profile · (2) Motivation vs. Cognitive Overload · (3)
School/College/Work Systems · (4) Emotional Regulation & Stress Tolerance · (5)
Sleep, Screens & Brain Energy · (6) Independence Readiness · (7) Accommodation
Planning.

**Deliverables:** EF profile · academic/work support plan · daily routine template
· sleep & screen reset plan · accommodation summary · parent communication guide ·
self-advocacy script · follow-up plan.

**Desired outcomes:** better task initiation and follow-through · reduced home
conflict · improved regulation · understanding of ADHD/AuDHD/anxiety patterns ·
readiness for independence · confidence and self-advocacy.

## Track 3 — Adults (18–64)

**Focus, Burnout, Brain Fog, and Daily Function**

**Purpose:** Support adults who feel scattered, overwhelmed, forgetful, or burned
out. Many have compensated for years until stress, sleep, hormones, illness,
trauma, parenting, or metabolic issues expose EF vulnerabilities.

**Best fit:** brain fog · poor focus · forgetfulness · unfinished tasks · constant
overwhelm · burnout · low mental stamina · disorganization · missed
appointments/deadlines · emotional reactivity · anxiety-driven avoidance ·
suspected adult ADHD · sleep problems · hormonal/metabolic change · "my brain
isn't working like it used to."

**Core questions:** ADHD, burnout, brain fog, anxiety, depression, medical strain,
or a mix? Which functions are most affected? What lifestyle/medical issues reduce
capacity? What systems help? Therapy, medication, coaching, medical workup, or
accommodations?

**Program modules:** (1) Adult Cognitive & EF Mapping · (2) Burnout vs. ADHD vs.
Brain Fog · (3) Daily-Function Systems · (4) Brain Energy & Medical Optimization ·
(5) Emotional Regulation & Stress Recovery · (6) Work & Life Accommodations.

**Deliverables:** cognitive/EF summary · brain-fog contributor checklist · daily-
function system · medical optimization plan · lifestyle recommendations · work
accommodation suggestions · follow-up plan · referrals.

**Desired outcomes:** improved clarity, follow-through, and organization · reduced
overwhelm · understanding of brain fog vs. ADHD vs. burnout · functional
confidence · better medical/lifestyle support for cognition.

## Track 4 — Adults With Suspected ADHD, Autism, or AuDHD

**Late-Identified Neurodivergence, Masking, and Burnout**

**Purpose:** Support adults — often high-performing adults, women, and lifelong
maskers — who suspect ADHD, autism, AuDHD, sensory differences, or lifelong EF

**Best fit:** lifelong disorganization · chronic overwhelm · sensory sensitivity ·

**Core questions:** Which patterns (ADHD, autism, AuDHD, anxiety, trauma, burnout,

EF supports, sensory/regulation needs, and accommodations would help?

**Program modules:** (1) Neurodivergent Pattern Mapping · (2) Masking &
Compensation Review · (3) Sensory & Nervous-System Regulation · (4) EF Systems for
Neurodivergent Adults · (5) Communication & Self-Advocacy · (6) Burnout Recovery &
Capacity Planning.

**Deliverables:** ADHD/autism/AuDHD pattern summary · EF profile · sensory support
plan · burnout recovery plan · work/home accommodation ideas · self-advocacy
language · referrals.

**Desired outcomes:** self-understanding · reduced shame · better EF and sensory
support · fewer burnout cycles · realistic routines · clearer communication of
needs · confidence in identity and functioning.

stability plan · trigger & coping map · daily routine template · appointment/med

**Desired outcomes:** better treatment engagement and attendance · improved
impulse control · stronger recovery routines · better regulation · reduced relapse
vulnerability · improved life skills and follow-through · confidence in recovery.

## Track 6 — Chronic Disease & Brain Fog

**Medical-Cognitive Stabilization**

**Purpose:** Support patients with chronic conditions experiencing brain fog,

**Best fit:** diabetes · insulin resistance · hypertension · sleep apnea · anemia ·

**Core questions:** Is the brain fog tied to medical instability? Which physiologic
systems are involved? Is the patient unable to follow the plan because of EF
dysfunction? What supports make the plan easier? What labs/referrals/lifestyle
changes restore capacity?

**Program modules:** (1) Brain-Fog Pattern Review · (2) Medical Systems Review · (3)
Care-Plan Simplification · (4) EF Support for Chronic Disease · (5) Lifestyle
Stabilization · (6) Care Coordination (PCP, behavioral health, specialists, CCM,
RPM, APCM, BHI, CoCM).

**Deliverables:** brain-fog contributor map · medical-cognitive care plan · lab
review summary · medication routine support · home monitoring plan · symptom
tracking tool · lifestyle stabilization plan · care-coordination plan.

**Desired outcomes:** understanding of brain fog · improved care-plan adherence and
symptom tracking · reduced overwhelm · better chronic-disease self-management ·
better team communication · improved cognitive stamina · confidence managing health.

# Module 6 — Standardized Screening & the Cognitive Profile

CharmEd Minds uses **standardized screening tools** to evaluate cognition, EF,
attention, emotional regulation, learning, and real-life functioning. The goal is
to identify whether symptoms relate to ADHD, autism/AuDHD, anxiety, depression,
trauma, substance-use recovery, sleep problems, medical contributors, cognitive
impairment, learning strain, or EF dysfunction.

> **This step is never one test.** It is a **screening battery matched to the
> person's age, concern, and population track.** And these are **screening**
> tools — they identify patterns and referral needs; they do **not** replace a
> full diagnostic evaluation when a formal diagnosis is required.

### A. Core Cognitive Screening Tools

| Tool | Best population | Measures |
|------|-----------------|----------|
| **CogniFit Cognitive Assessment** | Children→seniors | Attention, memory, processing speed, reasoning, perception, coordination, flexibility |
| **Creyos Health** | Teens, adults, seniors | Attention, memory, reasoning, planning, verbal ability, processing, EF |
| **NIH Toolbox Cognition Battery** | Children→adults (by version) | Working memory, processing speed, attention, inhibition, flexibility, episodic memory, vocabulary |
| **MoCA** | Adults & seniors | MCI screen; memory, attention, language, abstraction, delayed recall, orientation |
| **SLUMS** | Adults & seniors | Cognitive impairment screen; memory, attention, EF |
| **Mini-Cog** | Older adults / quick screen | Brief cognitive-impairment screen |
| **MindPrint Learning Assessment** | Children, teens, college learners | Learning profile, memory, processing speed, attention, EF, reasoning |

**Program use:** Use **CogniFit or Creyos** as the primary computerized battery;
add **NIH Toolbox** when a broader research-style battery is needed. Use
**MoCA/SLUMS/Mini-Cog** mainly for older adults or adults with decline concerns.

### B. ADHD & Attention Screening

| Tool | Best population | Measures |
|------|-----------------|----------|
| **Vanderbilt ADHD Diagnostic Rating Scale** | Children/adolescents | ADHD symptoms, impairment, oppositional/anxiety/depression indicators; parent + teacher versions |
| **Conners Rating Scales** | Children, teens, adults (by version) | ADHD, EF, behavior, learning, peer/family concerns |
| **ADHD Rating Scale-5** | Children/adolescents | DSM-5 ADHD symptom patterns |
| **SNAP-IV** | Children/adolescents | ADHD + oppositional symptoms |
| **ASRS v1.1** | Adults | Adult ADHD symptom screen |

### L. From Screening → the CharmEd Minds Cognitive Profile

Results are combined into a **CharmEd Minds Cognitive Profile** identifying:

- Primary cognitive **strengths** and **weaknesses**
- EF pattern · attention pattern · memory pattern · processing-speed concern
- Emotional-regulation concern
- Sleep or medical contributors
- Autism/AuDHD traits when present
- Recovery-related cognitive risks when present
- Chronic-disease self-management barriers
- Older-adult safety or memory concerns
- Recommended next steps

The profile then guides: parent/patient education · school/work accommodations ·
medical optimization · cognitive training · therapy/psychiatry/neuropsychology/
neurology referral · recovery supports · chronic-care-management supports ·
caregiver education · follow-up monitoring.

> **The purpose is not to label a person from one score** — it is to build a clear
> cognitive profile that guides education, treatment, accommodations, referrals,
> and confidence-building support.

# Module 7 — Medical & Lifestyle Contributors

A core CharmEd Minds principle: **optimize the body before (or alongside)
psychiatry.** Cognition can be suppressed by physiologic factors that are often
reversible. Non-clinical staff **screen and flag**; the Provider evaluates,
orders labs, and treats.

### 7.1 Contributors Reviewed Across the Lifespan

**Children:** sleep apnea / poor sleep · snoring or mouth breathing · chronic
congestion · recurrent ear infections / fluid · asthma · eczema / allergic
inflammation · iron deficiency / low ferritin · B-vitamin issues · vitamin D
deficiency · blood-sugar instability · gut symptoms · headaches · medication side
effects · chronic stress or trauma exposure.

**Adults:** sleep apnea · insomnia · iron deficiency · B12/folate · vitamin D ·
thyroid dysfunction · insulin resistance · blood-sugar instability · chronic
inflammation · autoimmune disease · perimenopause/menopause · testosterone issues ·
medication side effects · chronic stress · trauma burden · migraine · hypertension
/ vascular risk.

**Chronic-disease patients:** poor sleep quality · sleep apnea · anemia / iron
deficiency · B12/folate · thyroid dysfunction · blood-sugar swings · insulin
resistance · chronic inflammation · autoimmune activity · vitamin D deficiency ·
kidney disease · liver dysfunction · dehydration · electrolyte abnormalities ·
medication burden · pain · depression/anxiety · vascular risk.

**Seniors (reversible contributors):** B12 deficiency · thyroid dysfunction ·
anemia · sleep apnea · depression · anxiety · medication side effects ·
anticholinergic burden · alcohol use · poor nutrition · dehydration · infection ·
hearing impairment · vision impairment · uncontrolled blood pressure ·
diabetes/glucose instability · vascular disease · CKD.

### 7.2 Provider Lab Menu (as clinically indicated)

CBC · CMP · Vitamin D · B12 / Folate / MMA · TSH / Free T4 · A1C · ESR / CRP ·
autoimmune markers · homocysteine · hormones. 🩺 Ordered and interpreted by the
Provider only.

### 7.3 Lifestyle Levers

Sleep · nutrition (protein, meal timing, hydration) · movement · screen/device
habits · caffeine/stimulant use · blood-sugar stability · inflammation reduction ·
stress and trauma load.

# Module 9 — Communication, Scripts & Neurodiversity-Affirming Language

How we talk to patients *is* the intervention. We are **neurodiversity-affirming,
strengths-based, and non-shaming.**

### 9.1 Core Communication Principles

- **Behavior is communication**, not defiance or laziness.
- **Name the pattern, not the person** ("your brain works best with external
  reminders"), never a diagnosis from non-clinical staff.
- **Reduce shame** — frame struggles as brain-based and workable.
- **Give language first** — people need words for their experience before they can
  change it.
- **Cognition is like a muscle** — it fatigues but strengthens with consistent,
  right-sized practice.

### 9.2 Approved Scripts

**Intake introduction (Coordinator):**
> "Today I'll walk you through our CharmEd Minds cognitive intake. We'll talk about
> your learning or memory concerns and daily functioning, complete a few quick
> cognitive tasks, and set you up for digital testing. Then the provider reviews
> everything and builds your personalized plan."

**Assigning digital testing:**
> "You'll receive an email within about 5 minutes. Please complete the cognitive
> test within 48 hours. It takes about 25–40 minutes depending on age and modules."

**When a patient asks for a diagnosis (non-clinical staff):**
> "Great question. These are screening tools that help us map how your brain is
> working. The provider reviews all of it together and talks with you about what it
> means. I'll make sure that's on your list for your review visit."

**Encouraging a fatigued patient (training compliance):**
> "Executive function is like a muscle — it gets tired but grows stronger with
> consistent training. If you're feeling fatigued, try a shorter session today."

### 9.3 Working With Parents & Caregivers

- Frame behavior as communication and cognitive load, not character.
- Give concrete home routines and school/504/IEP language (children/teens).
- For seniors, educate caregivers on what is happening, how to support, and when
  further evaluation is needed.

### 9.4 Self-Advocacy Support

Help teens and adults build language for explaining their needs at school, work,
home, and in relationships (accommodation requests, communication scripts).

# Module 10 — Follow-Up, Progress Tracking & Escalation

### 10.1 The Review Rhythm

- **Weekly** — 97127 session + same-day documentation; homework and digital-training compliance tracked.
- **Monthly (Week 4)** — coordinator summary → **APD reviews first** → Provider updates plan and confirms medical necessity.
- **Quarterly (Week 12)** — full retest + digital battery; APD compiles the report; Provider interprets and adjusts (bills 96132); continuation decision made.
- **Discharge / maintenance** — discharge summary + final care plan; APD confirms docs complete; primary-care follow-up scheduled.

### 10.2 What We Track (Progress Tracking Sheets)

Working memory · processing speed · attention · flexibility · inhibition ·
emotional regulation · academic abilities · daily functioning · work performance ·
memory preservation — charted **weekly, monthly, and quarterly.**

### 10.3 Outcomes by Track (What "Better" Looks Like)

- **Children:** focus, school performance, regulation, routines, appropriate accommodations.
- **Teens/Young Adults:** task initiation, school/work follow-through, less home conflict, independence readiness.
- **Adults:** clarity, organization, reduced overwhelm, functional confidence.
- **Neurodivergent Adults:** self-understanding, EF/sensory support, fewer burnout cycles, clearer communication of needs.
- **Recovery:** engagement, attendance, impulse control, recovery routines, reduced relapse vulnerability.
- **Chronic Disease:** care-plan adherence, symptom tracking, self-management, cognitive stamina.
- **Seniors:** earlier identification, reversible-cause recognition, medication safety, caregiver understanding, safer routines.

### 10.4 Continuation Decision (Week 12)

🩺 Provider + APD choose: **continue · modify tier · maintenance · discharge · refer**
(neuropsychology, neurology, BHI, therapy, school psychoeducational testing).

### 10.5 Escalation Paths

- **Clinical/medical concern or red flag** → APD routes to Provider **same day**.
- **Formal diagnosis needed** (ADHD, autism, learning disability, dementia workup)
  → Provider refers for full diagnostic / neuropsychological / psychoeducational
  evaluation.
- **Crisis / safety** → follow BHW crisis protocol immediately.

# Appendix D — Staff FAQ

**Q: Can I tell a patient what their scores mean?**
A: You can report the scores and describe observed trends in plain language. You
cannot interpret them clinically or give a diagnosis — that's the Provider's role.

**Q: A patient hasn't done the home digital testing. What now?**
A: Coordinator follows up at Day 2–3; escalate at 48 hours. Testing should be done
within 48 hours of intake.

**Q: The child's family thinks it's "just ADHD." Do we skip the medical review?**
A: No. We screen for medical/sleep/nutrition contributors first (or alongside) —
"optimize the body before psychiatry." The Provider decides the pattern.

**Q: Which tier does a patient need?**
A: The Provider sets the tier (Bronze/Silver/Gold) based on needs and medical
necessity.

**Q: When do we refer out?**
A: When a *formal* diagnosis or higher-level evaluation is needed — neuropsychology,
neurology, BHI/therapy, or school psychoeducational testing — the Provider refers.

**Q: What do I do with a red flag?**
A: Tell the APD immediately. The APD routes to the Provider the same day. The APD
routes; the Provider decides. Safety/crisis → BHW crisis protocol immediately.
